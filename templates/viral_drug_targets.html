Viral Targets

Viral protein drug targets in structure-guided antiviral discovery

Viral drug targets are often evaluated through co-crystal ligand structures that reveal how inhibitors engage catalytic motifs, nucleic-acid handling regions, or allosteric pockets. Those structures can guide both classical inhibitor optimization and degrader-oriented follow-up.

Use V-LiSEMOD for

Target-first exploration, ligand context review, binding pocket inspection, and cautious warhead-readiness triage across curated viral structures.

Target classes

Examples of structurally important viral proteins

Proteases

Viral proteases remain central small-molecule targets because active-site and covalent or noncovalent inhibitor complexes often provide interpretable structural design cues.

Polymerases

RNA and DNA polymerase structures can support review of nucleotide analogs, non-nucleoside binders, and pocket-specific optimization strategies.

Reverse transcriptase

Reverse transcriptase complexes remain important for understanding antiviral ligand positioning, resistance-sensitive regions, and comparative binding behavior.

Integrase

Integrase structures and inhibitor complexes can reveal metal coordination, conserved binding patterns, and structural liabilities relevant to follow-up chemistry.

Spike, envelope, and fusion proteins

Where structurally supported, entry-associated proteins can provide ligand or interface context relevant to inhibitor design and mechanistic interpretation.

Helicases and accessory proteins

Additional viral proteins may become tractable when structural coverage, bound ligands, and interpretable pockets are available.

Why co-crystal structures matter

What structure-backed ligand data can support

Inhibitor optimization

Binding pose and interaction evidence help identify which ligand regions are likely to be protected versus more modifiable.

Ligand comparison

Cross-structure analysis helps reveal conserved contacts, context-dependent liabilities, and target-specific series behavior.

Binding pocket analysis

Researchers can inspect residue context, pocket openness, and local geometry before proposing structural changes.

Warhead selection

Some structures may better support warhead-oriented follow-up when solvent-facing atoms and attachment logic remain interpretable.

Degrader-readiness triage

Target structures can be prioritized for hypothesis-generating PROTACability review without overstating what structural data alone can prove.
