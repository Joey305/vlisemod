Viral PROTAC Design

Viral PROTAC design and antiviral degrader design

Viral PROTAC design refers to the careful attempt to adapt a viral target binder into one part of a bifunctional degrader concept. That step is challenging because a useful antiviral inhibitor is not automatically a useful degrader warhead.

Important limitation

V-LiSEMOD does not validate degradation; it prioritizes structures and ligand contexts for follow-up review.

Why this is hard

Viral targets create special design constraints

Viral proteins can offer compact binding pockets, transient interfaces, conformational variability, and uncertain cellular accessibility in degrader-relevant settings. Even when a ligand binds well in a co-crystal structure, that does not mean it can tolerate linker growth or recruit an E3 ligase productively.

A degrader concept has to preserve enough binding affinity and pose fidelity to keep the viral target engaged while also opening a path toward ternary complex formation, ubiquitination, and cellular degradation. Each of those later steps remains uncertain from structure alone.

Key design questions

  • Does the ligand binding pose expose a plausible attachment point?
  • Are any ligand atoms solvent-exposed enough to support exit-vector logic?
  • Could linker attachment preserve essential interaction context?
  • Is target lysine accessibility worth follow-up review?
Evidence layers

What a viral degrader concept needs beyond simple binding

Ligand binding pose

The observed pose helps define which atoms are buried, which interactions appear critical, and which regions may tolerate limited chemical growth.

Solvent-exposed ligand atoms

Exposure mapping can highlight atoms or moieties that remain outward-facing after binding and therefore may be more compatible with modification.

Exit-vector logic

Attachment is not just about exposure. The direction of growth matters for sterics, pocket escape, and downstream linker routing.

Linker attachment

Chemical tractability, synthetic feasibility, and preservation of target engagement all matter when moving from binder to candidate warhead.

Target lysine accessibility

Accessible lysine context may support follow-up review, but it does not prove productive ubiquitination will occur.

E3 recruiter selection

Recruiter choice can shape geometry, permeability, selectivity, and feasibility, making downstream partner selection a major source of uncertainty.

Ternary complex uncertainty

Even promising structural cues cannot guarantee that a productive ternary complex will form or persist in cells.

How V-LiSEMOD helps

Early triage before deeper modeling or experimental work

V-LiSEMOD helps prioritize viral structures for follow-up by combining ligand context, interaction evidence, solvent exposure, and target-side cues into a transparent review workflow. It is best used to narrow candidate structures and frame design conversations before medicinal chemistry, ternary modeling, or biological testing.
